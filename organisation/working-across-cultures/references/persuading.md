# Making a case, a proposal or a presentation to an audience from another culture

**44. Choose the order of argument for the audience in front of you, then ask the model for that version.** (Persuading
scale: principles- versus applications-first, chapter 3: *holds*.) Audiences still judge a case by the order their
schooling made habitual; a model only makes switching cheap. Principles-first audiences want the reasoning before the
conclusion, applications-first audiences the conclusion and the practical case first.

**45. Show principles-first audiences the method, and say how a model did the analysis.** (Principles-first audiences:
method before conclusions, chapter 3: *strengthens*; Show the method at work, chapter 3: *bends*.) When a model did
the analysis, everyone should ask how. Show the method working in the real business, because agents make
demonstrations cheap.

**46. Give the why with every request, to people and agents alike.** (Why versus how, chapter 3: *strengthens*.)
Principles-first colleagues need it, and so does every agent.

**47. For a mixed audience, cycle principles and examples live, and in writing give each reader their order.** (Mixed
audiences: cycle principles and examples, chapter 3: *bends*; Mixed reasoning styles slow global teams, chapter 3:
*bends*.) A model can reorder your argument for each reader; check that the summary kept the reasoning, and give the
meeting patience.

**48. Give holistic thinkers the whole picture before their slice.** (Big picture first for holistic thinkers, chapter
3: *strengthens*; Holistic and specific thinkers misread each other, chapter 3: *strengthens*.) AI action items hand
each person only their part and can cut the context that holistic thinkers treat as the point.

**49. Read a principles-first audience's challenges as interest.** (Welcome challenges as the antithesis, chapter 3:
*holds*.) A board that attacks the reasoning is engaging with it; answer the challenge, not the tone.

**50. For speed, sometimes leave the work with the local team.** (Sometimes leave Rome to the Romans, chapter 3:
*holds*.) Machine translation removes the language cost, not the culture cost.
