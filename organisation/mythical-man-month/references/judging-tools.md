# Weighing a tool, a productivity claim or a metric

**Ask three questions of any tool that promises a leap.** (No silver bullet, chapter 16: *bends*; the
three questions: *holds*.) Which problem does it address? Is that problem essence (deciding what the
system should be) or accident (expressing it)? Is the advance a revolution or an increment? Agents are
the strongest candidate yet, because they touch some of the essence, and a tenfold gain in
productivity, reliability and simplicity TOGETHER has not been shown.

**The gain is capped by the share of the work the tool cannot do.** (Essence and accident, chapter 16:
*bends*.) With agents that share is deciding what is wanted and judging whether it was delivered.
Estimate that share for the user's own work before promising a number.

**Measure the whole job.** Track reviewed changes, defects and stability. Do not report lines of code
or agent hours (constant statements per year, chapter 8: *breaks*). Do not extrapolate a demo to a
product (the programming systems product, chapter 1: *bends*; garage productivity does not transfer:
*strengthens*). Generalising, testing, documenting and integrating are still most of the cost, and
agents are fastest where the code is simplest (chapter 8: *holds*).
