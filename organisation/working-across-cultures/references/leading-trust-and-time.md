# Leading across levels of hierarchy, building trust, and working across time zones

## Leading

**28. Choose your signals of authority before a model chooses them for you.** (Aura of authority: signals of power,
chapter 4: *strengthens*.) When a model drafts your messages, it sets how formal, how deferential and how commanding
you sound. Decide that for each team, then check the drafts.

**29. Expect a hierarchical team to wait for the boss's direction, whatever the tools strip out.** (Hierarchical
leadership: deference, not tyranny, chapter 4: *holds*; The Leading scale: egalitarian to hierarchical, chapter 4:
*holds*.) A tool can drop honorifics; it does not change what people expect of a boss.

**30. Where the boss is rarely challenged, check a model's answer before you relay it.** (Paternalistic, 'father
knows best' leadership, chapter 4: *strengthens*.) A model's confident error passed on by the boss goes unchallenged
too.

**31. Agree whom to contact and whom to copy, and write it into every agent's brief.** (Agree level-hopping protocols
up front, chapter 4: *strengthens*; Level-hopping and copying etiquette by culture, chapter 4: *bends*.) Tools make
skipping a level effortless; whether it offends still depends on the culture. An agent sending on your behalf knows
your etiquette only if you write it down.

**32. Translate the words, then adapt the leadership.** (No single European leadership culture, chapter 4:
*strengthens*.) One message in every language localises the words, not the style of leading behind them.

**33. Agree objectives and step back with an egalitarian team, on the agents' faster cycle.** (Management by
objectives for egalitarian teams, chapter 4: *bends*.) Say openly how progress is seen, since agent work shows its
state all the time.

## Trust

**34. Trust from the head now rests on results you have checked.** (Cognitive trust: trust from the head, chapter 6:
*bends*.) Polished writing no longer proves skill.

**35. Read friendliness and warmth from what people do.** (Peach cultures: friendliness is not friendship, chapter 6:
*strengthens*; Coconut cultures: warm once through the shell, chapter 6: *bends*.) Friendliness never meant
friendship, and now it may not mean the sender wrote it. When every message sounds warm, judge a reserved colleague's
warmth by their actions.

**36. Ask for the introduction.** (Wasta: introductions through your network, chapter 6: *strengthens*.) When anyone
can send a polished cold e-mail, a friend's introductory call is worth more.

**37. Match the social talk in their original message.** (Match the other side's social talk, chapter 6: *bends*.) A
summary strips it and a drafted reply may flatten it; answer the person, not the summary.

**38. Show your unpolished self.** (Show your nonprofessional self, chapter 6: *strengthens*; Build on common
interests, chapter 6: *bends*.) When every message comes out polished, the unpolished self over a meal or a call is
what earns trust. An agent can suggest common ground; the trust comes from the hours spent on it together.

## Time

**39. Expect lateness to stay cultural, and ask how firm a date really is.** (Scheduling scale: linear- to
flexible-time, chapter 8: *holds*; Clashing planning horizons, chapter 8: *strengthens*.) A drafting tool gives every
deadline the same firm "by Friday". When a model writes the yes, ask how firm a date six months out really is.

**40. Write the team's time rules down, for reply windows and hand-offs as much as for meetings.** (Framing strategy:
an explicit team time culture, chapter 8: *bends*.) With recorded meetings, lateness moves from arrival to replies.

**41. Let a meeting branch, but write down the new plan before the agents run with the old one.** (Flexible-time:
elastic time, branching meetings, chapter 8: *bends*; Linear-time: one thing at a time, chapter 8: *bends*.) Keep the
agenda and the clock for people; agent work arrives in parallel and out of order.

**42. Before you trust an AI judgement of who is efficient, ask whose clock it uses.** (Each side sees the other as
inefficient, chapter 8: *strengthens*.)

**43. Give relationships time the tools cannot give.** (Relationships before the clock, chapter 8: *strengthens*.)
When a model can write the message, the time you spend is what proves the relationship.
